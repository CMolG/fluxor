# Monetization after acquisition: closing the funnel without breaking what attracts

Principle: monetization is placed **after the value and at the point of maximum intent**. What brings traffic stays free and visible; the next step, the one the user already wants to take, is what gets monetized. An article that ends in a paywall or a tool that demands a card to show the result destroys the strong element that sustained them.

## 1. From strong element to model

| Strong element | Models that fit | Insertion point |
|---|---|---|
| Educational content (articles, guides) | lead magnet → newsletter → service or course; honest affiliate; newsletter sponsorship; consulting | at the end of the article or of the cluster: "apply it to your case" |
| Free tool | freemium (usage limits, pro features), pay-per-use or credits, paid API/MCP, white label, extended report | after the result: save, compare, extend, automate, repeat |
| Data, listings, comparators | data subscription, premium alerts, qualified leads to third parties (marketplace), programmatic pages + sponsorship | when asking for an update, an alert or an export |
| Community, engagement, comments | membership, events, cohorts, member-only content | after participating several times (demonstrated return) |
| Services (freelance, agency, consulting) | discovery call, free or low-cost entry audit, fixed packages, retainer | after the content or tool that demonstrates judgment; never on a cold first visit |
| SaaS product | trial (with or without card), freemium, usage- or seat-based plans | after the activation event |
| Portfolio / personal brand | hiring, collaboration, training, mentoring | after the case study or demo that proves the capability |

## 2. Models: when, risk, metric

| Model | Choose when | Main risk | Closing metric |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Subscription** | recurring value (data, a tool used every week, continuous content) | churn if use is not truly recurring | conversion to paid, monthly churn, LTV |
| **Pay-per-use / credits** | sporadic but high-value use; real marginal cost per use (AI, compute) | volatile revenue; friction on every purchase | revenue per active user, repeat rate |
| **Freemium** | the tool wins with mass adoption and clear pro features exist | free tier too good (nobody pays) or too poor (nobody enters) | activation, free→paid (2-5 % indicative) |
| **Trial** | value is perceived within days and onboarding exists | trial without activation; drop-off when the card is requested | trial→paid (10-25 % without card, 40-60 % with card, indicative) |
| **Lead-gen / consultative sale** | high ticket, B2B, bespoke service | unqualified leads that consume time | visitor→lead (1-5 %), lead→customer (5-15 % B2B), sales cycle |
| **Productized services** | freelancer/agency wanting to scale without selling hours | poorly defined scope | conversion of the offer page, margin per package |
| **Digital product** (template, course, ebook) | strong content people want in an applicable format | piracy, low perceived value | conversion, refunds |
| **Affiliate** | you recommend tools you actually use and the content proves it | loss of trust if opaque | CTR to affiliate, revenue per reader |
| **Sponsorship** | defined, recurring audience (newsletter, section) | dependence on few sponsors | effective CPM, renewal |
| **Display advertising** | only with massive informational traffic and no other model | degrades UX, CWV and trust; incompatible with positioning a service | RPM; watch LCP/CLS |
| **License / API / MCP** | other products want to integrate your capability | support and SLA | revenue per integration, retention |

Percentages are indicative and vary widely by sector; they are used to spot anomalies, not as targets. Targets come from the site itself.

## 3. Designing the close: activation first

1. **Define the moment of value** ("aha"): the action after which the user understands what they gain (seeing their number, receiving the document, seeing the first real result). It is a measurable event.
2. **Define the activation event** and *time-to-value*: what a user who later converts does that one who abandons does not, and how long it takes. Without data, an explicit hypothesis and measurement from day one.
3. **Place the offer after activation**, with the benefit tied to what just happened ("save this result and get the breakdown", "automate this check every week").
4. **Shrink the payment step**: price visible beforehand, one recommended plan, one-step payment, no prior registration when possible (gateways with hosted checkout), automatic invoice.
5. **Recover**: follow-up email to those who activated and did not pay (with the value, not with pressure), cart or trial reminder with what they will **actually** lose, a call offer on high tickets.
6. **Retain and expand**: onboarding through to the second value, continued use measured, upgrade when usage hits the plan limit (transparent notice), referrals with a symmetric incentive.

## 4. Price as a hypothesis

- **Value-based**: price anchored to what the user saves or gains, not to cost. Compute a concrete case and put it on the page.
- **Three plans** with one recommended; the middle plan is the one you want to sell; the top one anchors and serves those who truly need it. A useless decoy plan is a dark pattern.
- **Annual with a real discount** and monthly without a trap; the annual price shown as total and as monthly equivalent, both.
- **Price test** before fixing it: simplified Van Westendorp survey (at what price would it be so cheap you'd doubt the quality? expensive but you'd pay? so expensive you wouldn't?) to 20-30 activated users; or two prices over two periods with equivalent traffic.
- **Transparency**: price, taxes and renewal visible before asking for data; cancellation in the same number of clicks as sign-up and without a call; notice before every annual renewal. This is EU consumer law as well as long-term retention.

## 5. Minimum unit economics

Before scaling acquisition, compute:

- **CAC** = acquisition cost (time included, valued) / new customers.
- **LTV** = average margin per customer and period × average lifetime (1 / churn).
- **LTV / CAC ≥ 3** as a general rule; **payback ≤ 12 months** for subscriptions.
- **Margin per use** if there is a marginal cost (AI, compute): price per use > cost per use × 3, or usage limits that guarantee it.

If the numbers do not work, the lever is price, cost or retention, not more traffic.

## 6. Legal and ethics (EU)

- Email marketing: explicit consent, double opt-in recommended, one-click unsubscribe, documented legal basis (GDPR).
- Cookies and conversion tracking: prior consent for anything non-essential; consent mode biases the figures, remember it when measuring.
- No dark patterns in payment or cancellation (DSA art. 25 for platforms; consumer law for everyone): no fake urgency, no pre-selected extras, no hidden "cancel" button, no drip pricing.
- Affiliate links and sponsorship clearly disclosed within the piece itself.
- Compliant invoicing (check the current e-invoicing and verifiable-billing-software obligations of the user's jurisdiction before recommending a gateway).
