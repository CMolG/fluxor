## 1. Headlines

The headline decides whether anything else is read (Ogilvy, Caples). On a landing page it also
decides whether the visitor stays.

**What a good landing-page headline does:**

- Promises a specific outcome the reader wants, or delivers news.
- Flags the right reader (Ogilvy: "flag down the readers who are prospects").
- Matches the ad, query or link that brought them.
- Passes Harry Dry's three questions: can I visualise it, can I falsify it, can nobody else say it?
- Is plain. Riddles and puns cost the reader effort, and only curiosity stacked on top of
  self-interest works (Caples).

Length is secondary. Ogilvy found longer headlines with news and information outsold short ones.
On mobile, check that it fits the first screen with the sub-headline and the CTA.

**Angles.** Write 5–10 headlines across angles, then pick. These are angles, not templates to fill:

| Angle | When it fits | Example shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Outcome** | Solution-aware; clear, desirable result | "Get paid in days, not months" |
| **Outcome without pain** | The main objection is effort or a known downside | "Blister-free long runs, without breaking in new socks" |
| **News / new mechanism** | Crowded market (sophistication 3+), a genuinely new way | "Reminders that write themselves in your voice" |
| **Pain / before** | Problem-aware; the pain is vivid and common | "Still chasing clients to pay?" (only when the answer comes right after) |
| **Identity / for whom** | Stage-5 skeptical markets; niche audiences | "Invoicing for freelancers who hate chasing money" |
| **Offer** | Most-aware traffic | "Team plan: unlimited staff, $49/month, 30 days free" |
| **Specific proof** | You have a striking, typical, sourced number | "Managers using Rotawise publish the rota in a median 11 minutes" |
| **Question** | Only when the reader would answer "yes, that's me" | "Is your best client also your latest payer?" |

**Pick** the one that is most specific, most believable and most clearly only yours. Keep two
runners-up as A/B test candidates. Put the product category somewhere in the hero (headline or
sub-headline), so the visitor can file it.

## 2. Sub-headlines

The sub-headline does the job the headline can't:

- what it is (the category);
- how it delivers (the mechanism);
- for whom.

One or two sentences. Peep Laja's check is "We help X do Y by doing Z." Don't paste it in, but the
sub-headline should answer those three.

> Headline: Next week's rota, done in 10 minutes.
> Sub: Rotawise builds the staff schedule for your restaurant from everyone's availability and
> sends it to their phones, so Sunday nights stop disappearing into a spreadsheet.

## 3. Body copy

- **Write to one reader**, in second person, about their situation. Use "we" for promises and
  proof ("we'll import your last 12 months").
- **Lead with the conclusion** (inverted pyramid). Each paragraph gets one idea, 1–3 sentences.
  Readers scan headings and numbers first (NN/g).
- **Section headings carry the message on their own.** "Reminders that sound like you", not
  "Features". Someone reading only headings should get the pitch.
- **Plain words at a low reading grade.** Unbounce's 2024 data ([LS], correlational): pages at a
  grade 5–7 reading level converted at a median 11.1%, vs 5.3% for professional-level copy.
  Needless complexity makes the writer seem less intelligent (Oppenheimer 2006). Expert B2B
  audiences can take technical terms they actually use, but not jargon.
- **Specific.** Numbers, names, times, places, mechanisms. Replace every adjective you can with the
  fact that justifies it.
- **Short opening sentences** (Sugarman's slippery slide), so each sentence earns the next.
- **Show the bright side.** Name the problem briefly in the reader's words, then spend most of the
  page on the outcome (Hopkins).
- **Cut** anything that doesn't raise desire or lower effort, confusion or risk.

## 4. Benefits, features and proof in the same line

The unit of a benefit section is **outcome → feature that causes it → proof**. The reader shouldn't
have to translate a feature into a benefit.

- Weak: "Smart scheduling. AI-powered shift planning with best-in-class accuracy."
- Strong: "**Stop texting people to cover shifts.** When someone drops a shift, Rotawise offers it to
  staff who are free and qualified, and the first to accept gets it. Most gaps are filled in under
  an hour."

Pattern for bullets: **bold outcome** + one sentence of how + a number or name where available.
3–6 blocks; each can also answer one objection.

## 5. Testimonials

- Only real quotes, verbatim, with permission (see `proof-and-compliance.md` §3).
- Pick for **specificity** (a number, a before/after, a named situation) and **similarity** to the
  target reader.
- Put a short headline above it drawn from the quote itself: "From 40 days late to 10".
- Attribute fully: name, role, company or city, a photo if allowed.
- Place it next to the claim it proves or the objection it answers.
- Ratings: "4.7/5 from 186 reviews on G2", linked to the source.
- When the user's testimonials are vague, keep them vague (don't embellish) and suggest how to
  collect better ones.

## 6. CTAs and microcopy

- **Button = what they get**, often in first person: "Start my free trial", "Build my first rota",
  "Get my first pair", "Save my seat for the June course". Avoid "Submit", "Learn more", "Click here".
  "Get started" is flat: say started with what.
- **Risk reducer under the button:** "14 days free · no card · cancel anytime". Keep it to the true
  terms.
- **Same primary CTA label throughout.** Repeat it after proof, after pricing and at the end.
- **Secondary CTA** for people not ready yet ("See how it works, 2 min"), visually quieter.
- **Microcopy** answers the anxiety at the point it arises:
  - next to an email field: "No spam. Unsubscribe anytime.";
  - near a price: "Billed yearly. Cancel anytime.";
  - on a form: why you need a field.

## 7. Pricing copy

- Name plans by who they're for ("Solo", "Studio"), and give a one-line "best for" under each.
- Describe the differences as outcomes ("Your logo on staff messages" rather than "White-label: ✓").
- Mark the recommended plan with the reason ("Most freelancers pick this").
- Put the value frame near the price, with the math (see `value-perception.md` §4).
- Put trial, guarantee and cancellation terms directly under the plans.
- A short FAQ right after pricing: "Can I switch plans?", "What happens after the trial?", "Do you
  take a cut of payments?"

## 8. FAQs and objections

- Source the questions from real objections: reviews, sales calls, support, what the user says
  people ask. The FAQ is where the remaining objections get answered, not a dumping ground.
- Write questions in the buyer's words ("Will my clients think it's rude?"), and answer directly
  in the first sentence, with proof.
- Order by how often the objection blocks a purchase. The usual suspects: price/worth it, will it
  work for me, time and effort to set up, switching, security/data, contract/cancel, support.
- Five to eight questions is typical. Link longer detail elsewhere.

## 9. The editing passes

Edit in separate passes, one concern at a time. These are adapted from the Copyhackers "sweeps"
approach.

1. **Clarity:** could a stranger say what it is, who it's for and what to do next? Remove jargon.
2. **So what?:** after every claim, ask "so what?" until you reach an outcome the reader cares about.
3. **Prove it:** every claim has proof next to it, or it gets softened or cut. Check each against the
   claims register.
4. **Specificity:** replace adjectives with numbers, names, times, mechanisms.
5. **Reader focus:** "you" over "we"; the reader's words over yours.
6. **Risk:** is every anxiety answered before the CTA it would block?
7. **Cut:** remove every sentence that doesn't raise desire or lower effort, confusion or risk.
8. **Lint:** run `scripts/copy_lint.py` with `--facts` and resolve its findings.

## 10. Before/after examples

**SaaS hero (scheduling tool for clinics)**

- Before: "Revolutionize your practice with our all-in-one AI-powered scheduling platform."
- After: "Fill last-minute cancellations automatically. When a patient cancels, [Product] texts
  your waitlist and books the first reply, usually within 15 minutes. [F3]"
- Why: an outcome in place of a category boast; a mechanism; a specific, sourced number.

**E-commerce product (merino running socks)**

- Before: "Premium quality socks made with the finest materials for ultimate comfort."
- After: "Run 20 km without a blister. 18.5-micron merino, a seamless toe and a heel cup that stays
  put. 4.8★ from 1,130 runners. [F1, F4, F7]"
- Why: the outcome runners want; features as causes; proof with a count.

**Service (bookkeeping for restaurants)**

- Before: "We are a passionate team of experts dedicated to providing excellent service."
- After: "Your books closed by the 5th of every month, by accountants who only work with
  restaurants. You'll see food cost % and labour % before your next supplier order. [F2, F5]"
- Why: reader focus; a specific deliverable and date; a niche authority signal.

**CTA**

- Before: "Submit"
- After: "Get my free menu-costing template" + "No card. Takes 30 seconds."

**Testimonial**

- Before: "Great product, highly recommend! — J."
- After: keep the real quote as it is, and ask the customer:

  > "Could you tell us what changed after you started? Roughly how much time or money, and how fast?
  > Can we use your name and role?"

## 11. Words and patterns to avoid

These make copy sound like every other page, and like it was written by a machine:

- **Hype and fog:** revolutionary, game-changing, cutting-edge, next-generation, world-class,
  best-in-class, seamless, robust, powerful, leverage, empower, unlock, supercharge, streamline,
  all-in-one, "the future of", "reimagine", "elevate", ecosystem, solution.
- **Company-centred openings:** "We are a passionate team…", "Our mission is…", "Welcome to…".
- **"Powered by AI"** as a benefit. Say what the AI does for the reader.
- **Stacked adjectives** and triplets of vague benefits ("fast, simple and powerful").
- **Exclamation marks and emoji in headlines.**
- **Unanswerable questions** ("Ready to take your business to the next level?").
- **Empty social proof** ("Loved by thousands", "Trusted by leading companies") without a real,
  specific number or names.
- **Fake conversational filler** ("Let's face it…", "Here's the thing:").

`copy_lint.py` catches most of these (English and Spanish lists). It can't catch vagueness that
uses ordinary words; the editing passes do.

## 12. Other languages

Write natively in the page's language. Don't translate English formulas.

- **Spanish:**
  - Choose tú or usted to fit the audience and brand. Consumer and startup brands in Spain
    mostly use tú; some Latin American markets and formal B2B prefer usted. Be consistent.
  - Avoid anglicisms when there's a natural word.
  - Use local number formats (4,8 · 1.240 · 24 €) and local legal terms (IVA incluido, derecho
    de desistimiento de 14 días in the EU).
- **Currency and number formats** follow the locale. Prices include VAT for EU consumers.
- **Readability formulas** in `copy_lint.py` cover English (Flesch-Kincaid) and Spanish
  (Szigriszt-Pazos/INFLESZ). For other languages, judge by sentence length and plain vocabulary.
