# micro-sale-copywriting: Four-Part Feature Sales

Each feature is written as a micro-sale: a unit that sells one thing, in four parts, in that order. A non-technical reader gets its value in under five seconds.

## Rules

1. **Hook first.** Open with a pattern interrupt that names the concrete friction the reader has today. No greeting, no product name in the first line.
2. **Mechanism second.** Say how the feature removes that friction, in one or two sentences a buyer can repeat.
3. **Asymmetric proof third.** Set the rival's version of the same friction against the product's instant outcome. The comparison must be fair and must cite its source when it names a rival.
4. **Micro-close last.** End with one action the reader can take now. Two actions in one unit is an error.
5. **Five seconds.** Read the unit as a reader who has never seen the product. If the value is not clear inside five seconds, shorten the sentences until it is.
6. **No jargon.** A term the buyer would have to look up is replaced by the plain word for the outcome.

## Output

A `MicroSalesCopyDeck`: one block per feature, each with its four labelled parts.
